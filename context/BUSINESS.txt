BUSINESS CONTEXT — owner-stated unless labeled otherwise

The business sells products through organic social content rather than paid ads. Dropshipping is the fulfillment method. The owner has about a year of experience, has run many products and coaches in a mentorship. Historical self-report: roughly $100K total revenue; goal: consistent $100K revenue months. These are revenue figures, not profit, and not independently audited here.

The owner can personally run about 2–3 products and wants to grow toward roughly 7–15 through better creative output and a repeatable creator process. Improve and validate creative results first; full automation is not necessarily the goal.

A good product and a good video are the central inputs. Views create opportunity, but viewers must also care enough to buy. Keep attention, purchase intent, site conversion and sales distinct.

PRODUCT EVALUATION
A product should add value or emotional meaning; it need not solve a problem. Value includes identity, enjoyment, gifting and usefulness. Broad audiences offer reach, but a passionate smaller audience can also work.
Discovery usually begins on an Instagram burner account trained on organic sellers; TikTok is also used. Custom products with a minimum order quantity are sometimes considered.
Assess timing and competition. A fresh product–audience combination is attractive. Existing competition can be acceptable if there is a concrete advantage in content reach or buyer conversion; “we will market better” alone is weak evidence.
The owner uses revenue per million views as a practical measure: about $3K is good and $10K very good. These are reported working benchmarks, not hard thresholds, forecasts or website conversion percentages. Unknown revenue rates stay unknown.
Hypothetical arithmetic: 10 million views × $3,000 per million = $30,000 revenue. This is only a scenario, not a prediction.

VARIANTS
A variant keeps the underlying product and changes the design, ideally reaching a fresh audience. A different hook alone is not a product variant. Look for proven product types paired with passionate audiences under-served in organic. Demonstrated organic precedent in the relevant product category is stronger evidence than aesthetic fit alone.
Product selection may end in approve / pass / needs more evidence, with plain reasons. Do not invent scoring systems, market sizes, sales or saturation thresholds.

RESEARCH IS A SEPARATE TASK
When a product is already chosen, focus on how to run it: likely buyers, creator presentation, audience language, sounds, hooks and demonstration shots. Do not restart product approval unless asked.

Historical product examples include Puzzport (24-day travel puzzle calendar), Warmside (Star Wars heating robes), Colorcozy (paintable nativity blanket), PITSTOP/Cars calendars, Naughty List underwear calendars, model-car humidifiers and themed jeans. These are context examples, not this creator's active assignments or proof of current availability.
